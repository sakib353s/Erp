| 08 Cash & Bank | 22 / 22 | **the money desk is open** — the registry of accounts money can sit in is the chart of accounts itself, so nothing on these screens can disagree with the general ledger: a bank account is a real postable leaf under Current Assets with the bank, the number and the currency recorded, a wallet is the same thing with a provider instead of a bank (and a separate permission, because money leaves a wallet through a different door), and every figure shown is the sum of posted journal lines — cash in hand, in the bank, in the wallets, per branch or for the whole company, with a negative drawer flagged rather than hidden. Money moves through three documents and one posting service: a receipt (Dr the account it landed in, Cr what it was for, numbered MR), a payment (Dr the cost, Cr the account it left, numbered EX) and a transfer between the company's own accounts (Dr the destination, Cr the source, one entry for the pair, numbered CT, idempotent because a retried transfer that moved the money twice would leave nothing behind to notice it by). Both sides are always named by the operator rather than guessed from a rule — the person holding the bank SMS is the only one who knows which account the money landed in — and paying "to" a bank account is refused with a pointer to the transfer desk, because that is what it is. An account can be renamed and re-described freely, but its code and its type are fixed once money has moved through it: changing a bank account into a cash account does not re-describe yesterday's postings, it makes every report grouped by type quietly wrong, so a new account is opened and the money is moved. An account holding a balance cannot be closed — that is the one state from which nobody can say where the cash went. Each account's book is the general ledger filtered to it, opening brought forward, running balance per row, exportable as the same rows in CSV, which is the page a statement is checked against. The desk also proves itself against the institution: a bank or wallet statement is imported as one document — whole file or nothing, a bad row named by its line number and nothing stored, a labelled brought-forward row read and set aside rather than refused, and the same file refused twice because that doubles every line — then the period is frozen on a reconciliation row with the figures the proof was made of, the leftovers listed on both sides, and `difference` standing as the one number that must reach zero: the period's unexplained **opening** gap between what the bank says the account opened with and what the books carried. Naming a leftover by hand restates those lists and moves nothing, so the desk says so instead of implying the number can be argued down; the closing figure can be corrected while the period is open, because reading it off the wrong row is how this desk usually fails; and a reconciliation is signed by somebody other than the person who prepared it, after which it is history. Wallet reconciliation is the same desk under its own permission, manual by design, with the screen stating that no provider API is connected rather than shipping a button that could not work. The cheques are in the same desk, and the rule they are built on is one sentence: a cheque reaches the ledger when a bank pays it, and not before — so recording one, depositing it and presenting it move the register and nothing else, and clearing posts once, from the one service allowed to post. A cheque that bounced before the bank ever paid it has nothing to reverse, because the ledger never heard about it; one that failed after clearing reverses the original entry instead of editing it, since the original really did happen. A post-dated cheque is taken in and then refused for deposit, presentation and clearing before the date written on it, which is exactly what post-dating means; a cheque clears through a cash, bank or wallet account only and never "settles" another one of our own accounts, because that is a transfer. Reading the register, writing a cheque into it and saying a bank paid one are three separate keys — the same separation the statement desk keeps between the person who imports a document and the person who signs it off. The printed record carries the amount in words in English and Bangla, says on its face that it is not a cheque, and is filed with the checksum of exactly what was printed. The expense desk is in the same place and refuses to guess anything: a category *is* a ledger account, so recording a bill means picking "Office rent" and the ledger is told 5220; the credit side is the account the money actually left or payables resolved through the company's own posting rules, never a code written into the service. At or above the approval limit the expense waits and carries no journal entry at all — waiting must never look like posted — the person who recorded it can never decide it, and undoing a posted expense writes the answering reversal rather than editing the original. The recurring desk is the same refusal taken one step further into the future: a standing expense is stored as a *schedule* — payee, amount, the day it repeats on, the date it first falls due — and writing one posts nothing at all, because a plan is not a payment. On the day, the generator writes an ordinary expense through the same service a person would have used, which is what makes the rule hold: a generated rent bill meets the same approval gate, the same category-is-an-account rule and the same reversal path as a typed one, and automation never becomes a way round a signature — the actor is the schedule's author, who is therefore the one person who cannot approve what it produced. The date advances exactly once, guarded by a unique (schedule, date) pair, so running the generator twice is a no-op rather than a second month of rent; a monthly schedule keeps its day and clamps to the end of a short month (the 31st is the 28th in February and the 31st again in March), because a rent day is a day in a contract; a refusal leaves the date where it was, so a switched-off category leaves the month visibly unpaid instead of skipping it; and a schedule whose last date has passed stops itself rather than generating into a period nobody agreed to. The daily command does all of this before the working day and says what it generated and what it refused; the desk's own button is for when today's rent cannot wait for tomorrow. The petty cash desk closes the loop the expense desk opens: a float is its own account in the chart of accounts held by a named custodian, a voucher is a payment out of that account and a replenishment is a transfer into it, so the tin's balance on screen is the ledger's own figure and no spending is ever counted twice. Above the company's own petty cash limit the money is asked for before it is spent — nothing posts while the request waits, and the person who asked cannot be the person who answers. A float holding cash, or one with a request still waiting, cannot be closed. **The count desk is the one screen here that does not believe the ledger**: a drawer is emptied onto a table, the notes and coins are written down, and the figure is checked against what the books said the drawer held on that day — the ledger's own balance, frozen onto the count with the tolerance it was judged against, so a count reads the same next year as it did then. Only the difference posts (a shortage to Cash Over & Short, an overage to Other Income, both through the company's own posting rules), an exact count posts nothing at all, and a breakdown of notes that does not add up to the counted figure is refused rather than tidied up. At or above the company's tolerance the difference is recorded and not posted until somebody other than the counter approves it, because money that is missing must not be written off by the person who was holding it; a refusal has to say why. The rules that let a bank charge post itself are here too, and they are written the way the bank describes them — a fixed amount, or a percentage of what actually left the account, with a floor, a rhythm and a day of the month — because the whole point is that nobody has to notice the charge again next quarter. Writing a rule posts nothing: a tariff is not a transaction. On its day the run posts two lines and no party — Dr the bank-charge expense, Cr the account the money left — and the turnover it was computed on is frozen onto the row so the figure can be checked against the bank's own arithmetic. A quarter in which nothing left the account is refused rather than charged at zero, the same rule cannot charge twice for the same day, a rule whose arrangement has ended stops itself, and a wrong charge is answered with a reversal rather than an edit. The report family closes the module, and it is built on one refusal: a report may not disagree with the ledger. The expense report counts **posted** expenses only — a bill waiting for a signature is not in the ledger and therefore not in its totals, and it says so in a footnote rather than quietly including it — and it *proves* the tie instead of asserting it, comparing its own total against the debit the categories' accounts actually carry and naming any difference per account, both sides, rather than absorbing it. The cash book opens with the balance brought forward from before the window and closes at the account's own ledger balance rather than the last row added up; the bank book is the same reading for every bank and wallet side by side; and the cash flow is built from the **counterpart** of every posting that touched a money account, so a receipt is named by the account it came from however it was filed — with transfers between the company's own accounts reported apart and left out of income, because moving money between two pockets is not cash flow, and with the inflow and outflow groups adding up to the money accounts' own debit and credit totals exactly. The one page that is not the ledger's is the session variance, and it says so on the screen: a till's expected cash is its own arithmetic and a variance is what a manager chases, not a posting — the count desk is where a difference against the books is answered. §08 is complete. |

Two pre-existing defects on the documents desk fell out of writing the first test that ever touched it: the library page printed a relation called `uploadedBy` that does not exist (so `/app/documents` returned 500 for everybody), and `Document` had no `branch()` relation despite its branch column and scope. **And twelve document types now print through one reusable renderer** (§16-23/§16-24/§16-25): a single table answers, per type, which `document_types` row gives the paper its title, which permission a person needs, how to load the document, what the paper says and which papers it prints on — and one shell renders all of them, in A4 and on 80 mm thermal, with the logo, the seal and the signature inlined (so a saved copy stands alone), a watermark, English and Bengali label sets and tables that repeat their head across pages. The title is a claim, so `DocumentTitleService` owns it: a normal sale prints INVOICE, a statutory form keeps its own title, and a non-statutory type may not carry a tax title — that now raises instead of printing. The tax block follows the same rule (D10): no tax on the transaction, no VAT column. Every production files a copy whose checksum is the checksum of the stored bytes, bumps that document's version, and writes a history row (title, paper, copy locale, watermark, user, address, file) read back at `/app/documents/print/{type}/{id}/history` and `/app/documents/print-log` behind the new `documents.view_history` key; printing needs the document's own key, per type. `payroll` and `payslip` are **listed as not printable with the reason** — §10 has employees, attendance and leave, and no payroll run to print from — and answer 422 rather than emitting a blank sheet. A real defect fell out of the first test that ever pressed these doors: the printing desk read the title service's array as an object and returned 500 for everybody. **And the trail itself became checkable (§16-33/§16-34/§16-35).** The audit log had been written since the first slice and nothing could check it: no test in the tree had ever walked the chain. Writing the first one found three real defects. **The chain never verified — not once.** The recorder hashed the timestamp it was about to store (the company's wall clock) while the verifier hashed what the model casts the same column back to (an ISO-8601 instant in UTC): six hours apart, so every row failed its own hash and “tamper-evident” was a claim nothing could confirm. One canonicaliser now serves both halves (`AuditRecorder::stamp()`/`plain()`). **The viewer's branch limit did not hold**, twice over: a `null` inside `whereIn('branch_id', […, null])` becomes `or branch_id is null` (admitting every company-wide row of every actor, on the page whose point is trustworthiness), and `$attrs['branch_id'] ?? $context->branchId()` meant a company-wide fact could never be recorded as one, because `??` swapped in the acting branch. The scope is now one grouped `where` behind the company predicate, and an explicit null is honoured. And **six of the twelve printable types were gated behind permission keys never checked against the seeder** — one, `sales.payments.print`, did not exist, so every non-administrator got a 403 from a door that looked fine in local testing; a census test now walks the renderer catalogue against the `permissions` table. On top of that: `AuditVocabulary` derives 28 modules from the 286 recorded actions (checked against the source tree, not kept by hand) and translates the five pre-vocabulary names (`reconcile`, `pay`, `escalate`, `print_*`, `notify_*`) **on read only**, because those rows are chained history and rewriting one breaks every hash after it; `erp:audit:seal` writes down a closed month (count, ends, checksum) only after the chain verifies — never for a month still running, never as an empty seal — and `erp:chain-verify --archives` re-reads every seal, the only thing that can catch a row deleted from an old month; “Verify now” leaves a `security.chain_verified` row with the verdict, so a check that happened is distinguishable from a page nobody opened; and the printable audit report carries the reason, because a refusal without its why is not evidence of anything. **And the warranty register, fed by the delivery event itself (§16-16/§16-17/§16-18).** A warranty is the promise in this application a customer can hold the company to, so cover comes from the product's own policy row and a product with no policy is delivered with **no cover at all** — the desk says “no policy” rather than inventing twelve months, because a default is a promise the company never made. Activation happens inside the delivery event's own transaction and is keyed on the delivered line (`UNIQUE (company, source_line_key)`), so a re-delivered challan or a retried request writes nothing: the test walks a challan back to `dispatched` and delivers it again to prove it. Where no challan carried the goods the invoice is the delivery event, and it refuses to promise twice what the challan already covered. The dates are fixed when cover starts — `ends_on` is the delivery day plus the policy's months, clamped, so a one-month cover begun on 31 January ends on the last day of February (29th in a leap year) instead of rolling into March — and editing a policy afterwards changes future covers and moves nothing already granted. The **clock, not the column**, answers “is this covered”: `stateNow()` compares `ends_on` with today while `status` keeps the life cycle, which is why the register opens on its live lens and shows an expiring strip at 30 days. A claim never edits the cover — closing one means stating what was done (repaired, replaced, refunded, or refused), by whom and at what cost, only an honoured resolution marks the cover `claimed`, and a cover that has run out or was withdrawn refuses a claim with its reason, because a goodwill repair is an expense rather than a warranty. The §07 leaves (`Warranty Returns | Processing | Completed`) and `Product Warranty Config` are pinned to the real screens, and new keys `sales.warranties.view` / `sales.warranties.manage` separate reading the register from deciding a claim. The rest of §16 — service/technician portal (§16-01…§16-15), search, the notification centre, BI, backups, the API surface and the security alerts of 16-36 (whose severity list is already written) — is still to come. |

## Known deviations inside what *is* built (documented, not hidden)

* **The test suites have now been run — not all of them yet, and the ones that have were run for the first
  time.** Until this pass only a static harness ran over the tree (lint, Blade extraction, class references,
  route names); it proves syntax and wiring, and nothing about behaviour. Executing the suites under a real
  PHP 8.5 / Laravel 13.32 / PHPUnit 12 runtime found defects the harness structurally cannot see, and fixed
  them: the **cheque register answered 500** because `Cheque::STATUSES` referenced a `STATUS_ISSUED` constant
  that was never declared (the model had `DIRECTION_ISSUED`); **a fresh instance could not post a single
  journal entry**, because `ReferenceDataSeeder` — the seeder that creates the current fiscal year — was
  missing from the list of company-scoped seeders the first boot re-runs, so the chart of accounts existed
  with no open period, and the system roles were built before the permission catalogue existed, leaving them
  with no keys; **business permissions were never registered with the Gate**, so the twenty-nine form requests
  that ask `$user->can('…')` refused everybody but a super admin; `counterparty` was missing from
  `BusinessRecord`'s fillable list, so every contract and agreement lost the other party; `NoticeService`
  counted a notice's author as outstanding on their own acknowledgement ledger, making 100% unreachable; a
  payment could name another cash or bank account as its other side, which is a transfer wearing the wrong
  form; and `where('trip_date', '<=', $date)` silently dropped the **last day of every date range**, because
  Eloquent writes a `date` cast as `Y-m-d 00:00:00` and that string sorts after `Y-m-d` — the trip log's own
  page could not show today's trips. Suites passing today: Unit 14, CashCountDeskTest 13, CashBankDeskTest 9,
  ChequeDeskTest 10, AssetRegisterTest 36, BusinessRecordTest 23, MeetingBoardTest 18, NoticeBoardTest 13,
  TaskBoardTest 15, InventoryCoreTest 19, UtilityDeskTest 21, VisitorDeskTest 23, MarketingDeskTest 36
  (322 assertions). The remaining feature suites have the same treatment waiting for them, and it is the
  highest-value work left in the repository.

* **Dashboard containers** are answered by one read service (`App\Domain\Dashboard\Services\DashboardMetrics`)
  rather than the planned per-widget classes and per-widget `dashboard.widget.*` keys: a container is visible
  exactly when the caller holds the view permission of the module whose documents it reads, and each container
  reports `ok`, `empty` or `unavailable` — the last one naming what is missing. No panel is left naming an unbuilt
  module: the expiry panel (01-21) reads the batch register as of the §04-39 slice, and cash position's
  `unavailable` is a real statement about this company's chart of accounts, not about missing code. The plan, the shipped shape and the reasons are side by
  side in `docs/TRACEABILITY/01-dashboard.md`. While wiring it, a real defect was fixed: `WidgetSeeder` produced
  mixed-case container codes (`Todays_Sales`) while the translations table, the label keys and the dashboard all
  key off lowercase slugs — codes are lowercase now and the seeder prunes containers outside the catalogue.
* **Damage, loss and write-off** are two documents, not one (see `docs/TRACEABILITY/04-inventory.md`):
  entries state facts (damage moves goods into the `damaged` compartment with no valuation effect;
  a loss consumes the layers and posts Dr 5260 / Cr 1140), while the write-off is the decision that
  removes value and needs a second person. `damaged` and `quarantined` are compartments held outside
  sellable stock and are valued all the same — and the ledger's state machine (now on
  `StockMovement::applyDelta()`) was corrected here: an outbound movement from the damaged compartment
  used to decrement `on_hand` a second time. Nothing posts to insurance: 04-50 has no register to
  link a claim to.
* **Two alert readers exist.** `StockQuery::alerts()` came with the inventory core and
  reports a company-wide balance against thresholds; the alert screens read
  `ReorderService::alertRows()`, which resolves the policy that actually governs a product
  in a warehouse (a warehouse row beats the company-wide one) and adds the shortage and a
  suggested order quantity. The screens are the single source of truth for what the user
  sees; `StockQuery::alerts()` is left as a balance-only summary rather than deleted, so
  nothing that calls it breaks. Products with no active policy are never alerted.
* **Reorder suggestions** (04-56) are *not* auto-posting purchase orders, and there is no
  standing "auto reorder" switch: the alert's Order action opens a prepared purchase order
  for a human to approve, which is the honest behaviour until a schedule and a strict mode
  are configured (04-58 still open).
* **Purchase approval** runs on the direct permission `purchase.orders.approve` with a
  creator-cannot-approve rule in the service, not through the shared Workflow engine
  (`approval_requests`). Same seam as HRM leave approval; one call to re-point.
* **Over-receipt** is a hard refusal with the numbers in the error. The configurable
  tolerance of 03-29 is not implemented.
* **Three-way match** is computed on bill approval and stored with a human summary, but
  with no configurable tolerance it *records* a mismatch instead of blocking it — a real
  liability is never hidden, and the mismatch is shown in full on the bill.
* **Purchase returns** post through `posting_rules` (`purchase_return_posted` → Cr inventory
  1140, `purchase_return_expense_posted` → Cr purchases & services 5225, both Dr accounts
  payable 2110 with Cr tax payable 2120 for the input tax reversed). A return tied to a bill
  credits that bill in the same transaction (`credited_amount`), so `due_amount =
  total − paid − credited` is the single definition of what a bill still owes and the AP
  control account never disagrees with the sum of the bills.
* **Supplier payments** post through `posting_rules` (`supplier_payment` → cash 1110,
  `supplier_payment_bank` → bank 1120) into the shared `payments` table with direction
  `out`; a payment is always allocated to one posted bill, never left floating, and the
  same idempotency key can never pay twice.
* **Purchase bill posting** runs through `posting_rules` (`PurchaseCoreSeeder`): goods-backed
  bills debit Inventory (1140), direct/service bills debit Purchases & Services (5225),
  input tax debits Tax Payable (2120, net-VAT treatment) and the credit is Accounts Payable
  (2110). Approval is `purchase.bills.approve` with maker ≠ checker; the Workflow engine is
  still not wired to purchasing.
* **No outbox** anywhere in purchasing, so there is no "sent to supplier" state, no PO
  print/email and no reminder — the UI never claims any of them.
* **Goods receipt conditions** — only `batch_no` is captured per line; condition,
  expiry and bin allocation (03-34) are missing, so FEFO and the quality-reject report
  have no input yet.
* **Supplier contacts/bank** live on the supplier record as plain attributes: no
  multi-contact table, no encrypted bank sub-record, nothing described as verified.
* **Customer ledger** is derived from `invoices` + `payment_allocations` — the
  same rows the accounting engine posts — rather than reading `journal_lines`
  directly. Figures reconcile, but a true GL-side ledger view
  (`LedgerService::accountLedger` by AR control account) is still pending (05-07).
* **Statement/ledger printing** uses the browser print pipeline and the
  §18.2 PrintableDocumentFrame; a server-side PDF renderer (05-17 `DOC PDF EN/BN`)
  is not wired yet.
* **Credit-limit enforcement** is available as `CustomerService::creditProjection()`
  and `assertOrderable()`; the sales order/invoice entry points do not call them
  yet, so today the limit *warns on the CRM screens* but does not block a document
  (05-16 config `block|warn` decision still open).
* **Customer/supplier import** (05-03, 06-03) is not built; CRM export is (CSV,
  scope + filter aware).
* **Collections workflow** (05-09…05-15): assignment, follow-up log,
  promise-to-pay, legal notice and EMI plans are not built.
* **Bulk action reason/courier dialogs** on the orders screen are wired to the

* **HRM payroll** (10-25…10-33) is deliberately out of scope so far: no salary run,
