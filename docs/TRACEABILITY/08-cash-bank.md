# 08. CASH & BANK — Traceability

Baseline (`BL`) applies. Shared services: `CashBank\Actions\{OpenCashSession, CloseCashSession, RecordCashReceipt, PostCashPayment, TransferCash, ReconcileBank, ImportBankStatement, PostBankCharge, ClearCheque, BounceCheque, CreateExpense, ApproveRecurringExpense, RequestPettyCash, ApprovePettyCashRequest, ReplenishPettyCash, PostWalletTransaction}` · `CashBank\Services\{CashSessionService, ChequeService, PettyCashService, BankReconciliationService, WalletService}` · `Accounting\JournalPostingService` (all money movement), `DocumentRenderer` (receipts/cheque printouts), ApprovalEngine for expenses/payments above thresholds.

All cash/bank/wallet movement = GL postings; mobile wallets (bKash/Nagad/Rocket/Upay) are financial accounts with **manual reconciliation** (API = optional adapter, never faked).

## §08 as shipped so far

The desk, the books and the registry are one mechanism: **a money account is an
account in the chart of accounts that money can sit in**, so the registry adds
only what a ledger cannot say (which bank, which number, which provider) and
every position on the screens is the sum of posted journal lines rather than a
stored balance. `MoneyAccountService` owns the registry and the positions;
`MoneyMovementService` owns every way money moves — in, out, and between the
company's own accounts — and each movement posts through `JournalPostingService`
(the one path allowed to post) with a numbered document behind it: a money
receipt (MR), an expense voucher (EX) or a cash transfer (CT).

The two lists cannot drift apart, and that is enforced rather than assumed: the
migration backfills `instrument` from the `is_cash` / `is_bank` flags the standard
chart already carried, `MoneyAccountService` still finds a flagged account whose
column was never filled (a chart seeded *after* this migration is exactly that
case) and derives its instrument when asked, and every write to an account keeps
the column and the flags saying the same thing — a flagged account is filled in,
an instrument a screen set explicitly is left alone, and a group row is never a
money account. `CashBankDeskTest` pins the derived position; the migration and the
model hook are what keep `instrument` and the flags from ever disagreeing.

The second slice is the desk that proves the first one: a bank or wallet
statement is imported as one document (whole file or nothing), the period's
figures are frozen on a reconciliation row, and the leftovers on each side either
explain the whole gap between the bank's closing figure and the books or the
reconciliation cannot be signed off — and never by the person who prepared it
(08-08, 08-09, 08-12). What moved into that slice and is worth stating plainly:
`difference` is the period's **unexplained opening gap** (statement opening
against book opening), so pairing a leftover by hand restates the lists and
cannot move that number — the desk says so instead of implying otherwise, and the
closing figure can be corrected while the period is open, because reading it off
the wrong row is the commonest way this desk fails. Wallet reconciliation is
manual and the screen says no provider API is connected; no adapter is pretended
to exist.

The third slice is the money that has been promised but not paid: **the cheque
register (08-13, 08-14)**. Its whole rule fits in a sentence — a cheque reaches
the ledger when a bank pays it, and not before — so recording one, depositing it
and presenting it move the register and nothing else, and the clearing entry is
the service's single posting call, made once and never twice. A cheque that
bounced before the bank ever paid it has nothing to reverse, because the ledger
never heard about it; one that failed *after* clearing reverses the original
entry rather than editing it, because the original really did happen. A
post-dated cheque is taken in and then refused for deposit, presentation and
clearing before the date written on it, which is what post-dating means. Reading
the register, writing a cheque into it and saying the bank paid one are three
separate keys, for the same reason a statement is imported by one hand and signed
off by another. The printed record (08-14) carries the amount in words in both
languages this office writes in, states on its face that it is not a cheque, and
is filed as a document with the checksum of exactly what was printed.

Still PLANNED here, and why: **expenses,
categories, approval and recurring (08-15…08-19)** need the category↔account
mapping and the approval engine wiring; **petty cash (08-21)** needs the fund and
its replenishment journal; **cash count and session variance (08-05)** needs the
count sheet; **cash/bank/expense reports (08-20, 08-22)** are the reporting family
on top of what is now posted — the per-account book exists already (08-07), the
report centre family does not.
