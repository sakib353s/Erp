# 08. CASH & BANK — Traceability

Baseline (`BL`) applies. Shared services: `CashBank\Actions\{OpenCashSession, CloseCashSession, RecordCashReceipt, PostCashPayment, TransferCash, ReconcileBank, ImportBankStatement, PostBankCharge, ClearCheque, BounceCheque, CreateExpense, ApproveRecurringExpense, RequestPettyCash, ApprovePettyCashRequest, ReplenishPettyCash, PostWalletTransaction}` · `CashBank\Services\{CashSessionService, ChequeService, PettyCashService, BankReconciliationService, WalletService}` · `Accounting\JournalPostingService` (all money movement), `DocumentRenderer` (receipts/cheque printouts), ApprovalEngine for expenses/payments above thresholds.

All cash/bank/wallet movement = GL postings; mobile wallets (bKash/Nagad/Rocket/Upay) are financial accounts with **manual reconciliation** (API = optional adapter, never faked).

## §08 as shipped so far

The desk, the books and the registry are one mechanism: **a money account is an
account in the chart of accounts that money can sit in**, so the registry adds
only what a ledger cannot say (which bank, which number, which provider) and
every position on the screens is the sum of posted journal lines rather than a
stored balance. `MoneyAccountService` owns the registry and the positions;
`MoneyMovementService` owns every way money moves — in, out, and between the
company's own accounts — and each movement posts through `JournalPostingService`
(the one path allowed to post) with a numbered document behind it: a money
receipt (MR), an expense voucher (EX) or a cash transfer (CT).

The two lists cannot drift apart, and that is enforced rather than assumed: the
migration backfills `instrument` from the `is_cash` / `is_bank` flags the standard
chart already carried, `MoneyAccountService` still finds a flagged account whose
column was never filled (a chart seeded *after* this migration is exactly that
case) and derives its instrument when asked, and every write to an account keeps
the column and the flags saying the same thing — a flagged account is filled in,
an instrument a screen set explicitly is left alone, and a group row is never a
money account. `CashBankDeskTest` pins the derived position; the migration and the
model hook are what keep `instrument` and the flags from ever disagreeing.

The second slice is the desk that proves the first one: a bank or wallet
statement is imported as one document (whole file or nothing), the period's
figures are frozen on a reconciliation row, and the leftovers on each side either
explain the whole gap between the bank's closing figure and the books or the
reconciliation cannot be signed off — and never by the person who prepared it
(08-08, 08-09, 08-12). What moved into that slice and is worth stating plainly:
`difference` is the period's **unexplained opening gap** (statement opening
against book opening), so pairing a leftover by hand restates the lists and
cannot move that number — the desk says so instead of implying otherwise, and the
closing figure can be corrected while the period is open, because reading it off
the wrong row is the commonest way this desk fails. Wallet reconciliation is
manual and the screen says no provider API is connected; no adapter is pretended
to exist.

The third slice is the money that has been promised but not paid: **the cheque
register (08-13, 08-14)**. Its whole rule fits in a sentence — a cheque reaches
the ledger when a bank pays it, and not before — so recording one, depositing it
and presenting it move the register and nothing else, and the clearing entry is
the service's single posting call, made once and never twice. A cheque that
bounced before the bank ever paid it has nothing to reverse, because the ledger
never heard about it; one that failed *after* clearing reverses the original
entry rather than editing it, because the original really did happen. A
post-dated cheque is taken in and then refused for deposit, presentation and
clearing before the date written on it, which is what post-dating means. Reading
the register, writing a cheque into it and saying the bank paid one are three
separate keys, for the same reason a statement is imported by one hand and signed
off by another. The printed record (08-14) carries the amount in words in both
languages this office writes in, states on its face that it is not a cheque, and
is filed as a document with the checksum of exactly what was printed.

The fourth slice is what the company spends: **the expense desk (08-15…08-18)**.
Its shape comes from one refusal — this module never guesses a ledger account.
An expense category *is* a general-ledger account (08-17), so the person
recording the bill picks "Office rent" and the ledger is told 5220; a category
may only point at a postable expense leaf of the same company, and it is
deactivated rather than deleted, because last year's expenses still have to be
able to name it. The credit side is either the account the money actually left,
chosen by whoever paid, or payables resolved through the company's own posting
rules (`expense_posted`) — never a code written into the service. Recording an
expense, deciding it and re-pointing a category are separate keys, because the
mapping decides every future figure in the expense report. Above the approval
limit (a setting, copied onto the document as `approval_threshold` so a later
change cannot rewrite why a past signature was asked for) the expense waits and
carries no journal entry at all: waiting must never look like posted. The person
who recorded an expense can never decide it — the rule is in `ExpenseService`,
not in a hidden button — and undoing a posted expense writes the answering
reversal rather than editing the original, because the money really did leave.

The fifth slice is the expense that comes back every month: **recurring expenses
(08-19)**. It is a schedule and never a posting — `recurring_expenses` records
what recurs, when it next falls due and what it has produced, and nothing in the
desk touches the ledger at the moment a plan is agreed. On the day, the generator
writes an ordinary expense through `ExpenseService`, which is the whole design: a
generated rent bill meets the same approval gate, the same category-is-an-account
rule and the same reversal path as one somebody typed, and automation is never a
way round a signature. The actor is the person who created the schedule, so they
are structurally the one person who cannot approve what it produced. Two smaller
decisions carry most of the value. A monthly schedule keeps its day and clamps to
the end of a short month — the 31st is the 28th in February and the 31st again in
March — because a rent day is a day in a contract, not a rounding artefact. And a
refusal (a category that has been switched off, say) leaves the date exactly where
it was: the month stays visibly unpaid until a person puts it right, instead of
being skipped silently by a generator that moved on. A second run is a no-op
rather than a second month of rent — `expenses_recurring_unique` on (schedule,
date) is the referee, the date only moves once, and a schedule whose last date has
passed stops itself rather than generating into a period nobody agreed to. The
daily command (`erp:cash:recurring-expenses`, scheduled before the working day)
does the same thing without a browser and reports what it generated and what it
refused; the desk's own button is for when today's rent cannot wait for tomorrow.

The petty cash desk is the same money path seen through a much smaller hole. A
float is a real account in the chart of accounts held by a named custodian, so
the figure on the screen is the figure in the books — the desk keeps no running
total of its own precisely so the two cannot drift apart. A voucher is a payment
out of that account and a replenishment is a transfer into it, which is why
putting the money back never double-counts the spending already recorded voucher
by voucher. Above the company's own petty cash limit (a different number from
the expense limit, because a drawer of small money is judged by a different
scale) the money is asked for before it is spent: nothing posts while the request
waits, and the person who asked cannot be the person who answers. A tin holding
cash, or one with a question still hanging over it, cannot be closed.

The count desk is the one screen in this module that does not believe the ledger.
A drawer is emptied onto a table, the notes and coins are written down, and the
figure is compared with what the books said the drawer held **on that day**: the
ledger's own balance for the account, frozen onto the row so a count reads the same
next year as it did then. Only the *difference* posts — a shortage debits Cash Over
& Short and credits the drawer, an overage debits the drawer and credits Other
Income, the same two accounts the COD remittance desk uses, so the two desks cannot
disagree about what a short drawer means — and a count that matches the books posts
nothing at all, because there is nothing to correct. At or above the company's own
tolerance (`cash.cash_count_tolerance`) the difference is recorded and **not**
posted: money that is missing must not be written off by the person who was holding
it, so the counter cannot be the one who approves, and a refusal has to say why. A
count that does not match the books has to say why too, and a written-down
breakdown that does not add up to the counted total is refused rather than tidied
up — the whole point of writing the notes down is that somebody can add them up
again. One pending count per drawer, so nobody is asked to approve the same gap
twice; only an account whose instrument is cash can be counted, because a bank or a
wallet is proved against a statement rather than against a pile of notes.

Still PLANNED here, and why: **cash/bank/expense reports (08-20, 08-22)** are the
reporting family on top of what is now posted — the per-account book exists already
(08-07), the report centre family does not.
