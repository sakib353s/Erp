# 12. BUSINESS MANAGEMENT — Traceability

Baseline (`BL`) applies. Spec §22: all records are real database entities with status history and auditability.

**Built so far: the notice board (§12-12) and tasks & projects (§12-13)** — the two things an office runs on that are not documents. A notice stores the audience it was published to (everybody, roles, branches or named people) rather than recomputing it, so “we told the Dhaka outlet in October” survives a change of roster; it fans out in-app notifications through the one `NotificationCenter` with a stable dedupe key (re-publishing amends the board, it does not re-notify everybody), and an **acknowledgement is a row in `notice_acknowledgements` against a person**, written once — which is why it survives a refresh, a new login and an emptied inbox, and why the tracking page can name who has *not* read a policy instead of counting sent emails. The audience is honoured on the notice's own page, not just on the board: reading somebody else's notice by typing its URL is a 403, and a draft is a 404 for anybody who may not publish. Tasks are a state machine with a legal-transition map (`todo → in progress → blocked/review → done`, done reopenable, cancelled final); every move, assignment, due-date change and comment writes a `task_events` row *and* an audit event; “my tasks” and “everybody's tasks” are separate doors (`tasks.view_own` / `tasks.view_all`) so scope is a query rather than a flag a view might forget; the kanban's columns come from the state machine itself; and overdue is computed from the clock, never from a stored flag a nightly job might not have set. Projects are containers whose counts are computed from their tasks, with a code unique inside the company.

**Built this slice: the company's registers (§12-03, §12-04, §12-09, §12-10)** — the trade licence, TIN & BIN, the certificates, the contracts, the agreements, the brand assets, the insurance, the RJSC filings and the statutory duties. They are one table with a `kind`, not nine tables: `RecordsRegistry` says which fields each kind has, which date it tracks and what each column is called, so a licence and a policy cannot drift apart and a new kind of paper is a registry entry rather than a fifth copy of the same CRUD. Three facts are held to. The state of a record is **read from the clock** — `expired`, `expiring`, `valid`, `undated` — and there is no `state` column, because a register that is only true after the nightly job has run is a register that lies for part of every day. A **renewal** extends the expiry, refuses to move it backwards, and keeps the previous date in `business_record_events.meta`, so the number that was true last year is still a fact. A **recurring duty**, once done, sets its next date from its *deadline* rather than from the day the work was done — a return filed three days late is still due the same day next month — and steps over any cycles missed entirely so the next date is one somebody can act on. Evidence is attached, never copied: the paper stays in the document library with its checksum and its audit trail, and the record keeps a label saying what the file is doing there. Retiring needs a typed reason and deletes nothing; the renewals lens keeps “no date on file” as its own bucket rather than treating it as safe; the compliance calendar is drawn as the real weeks of the month (Sunday first) with each entry coloured by its own state; and `erp:business:compliance-alerts` runs every morning at 06:50 with two digests — what has lapsed, and what lapses inside the month — deduped per state, per company, per day, notifying the holders of `business.records.manage`.

Still PLANNED in this module: the company/branch rows (§12-01…§12-08 — company profile, branch CRUD and branch settings exist under other refs and are repinned when they are traced), meetings (§12-11), assets and vehicles (§12-14), utility bills (§12-15) and visitors (§12-16).
